# Restaurant and bar — why it works this way

## Who works where

Sebastian, 2026-10-04:

- **There is always one member of staff at the front desk, one in the kitchen and one in the restaurant or bar.** They swap places through the day, depending on who is closest or not busy.
- **They all use the system at the same time**, each on their own device.
- **Nothing is off limits to anyone who is staff.** Every screen is open to all of them.
- The flow: staff takes the order → sends it to the kitchen → the kitchen cooks → staff carries it to the guest → when the guest is done, they are sent to the front desk to pay.

## Order slips

How the staff work without the system: **they write an order on a paper order slip and give it to the kitchen** (Sebastian, 2026-10-04). Their feedback the same day shaped everything below.

- **It is called an order slip, not a tab**, because that is the paper the staff already use.
- **Every order slip has a number (OS no.).** The app gives it, counting up and never restarting.
- **The order reaches the kitchen on a screen, not on paper.** Sebastian, 2026-10-04: *"the staff taking orders doesn't have a way to print orders to give to the kitchen. only the front desk, so it doesn't make sense for the staff at the restaurant to go back and forth the front desk to print the order."* The only printer is at the front desk. He chose a screen in the kitchen over a second printer. When more is ordered on the same slip later, the kitchen is shown only what is new.
- **A slip stays open until it is paid; the next order after that starts a new slip.** The staff's complaint: the restaurant went on showing a room's items after they had been paid.
- **The same dish twice is `2 ×` on one row**, not a second row.

## Who can be charged

- **Anyone can have an order slip**, including diners with no room. The owner said walk-in diners are most of the restaurant's trade.
- **An order starts with a table, and the staff start it.** Sebastian, 2026-10-05, the two flows as he gave them: a guest walks in **without a room**, chooses a table, the staff take the order, give it to the kitchen, serve the food, and the guest is directed to the front desk. A guest walks in **with a room**, chooses a table, and it goes the same way — except that at the end they either go to the front desk or the bill is added to their room, "for them to pay whenever as long as they pay before they check out of the hotel."
- **A room is only asked for when the bill comes.** *"Not all guests want their bills added to the rooms."* So a table is not tied to a room while the order is taken.
- **The line of tables shows only what the restaurant still has something to do with.** Every guest in the hotel used to stand in it, ordered or not: *"having the tables and room always being shown feels like I'm always supposed to do something with them … even when they don't yet."* Then it was every open order slip, and he said it again the same day, of rooms standing there with an empty slip or with food served days before: *"I don't like the fact that these rooms and tables are being displayed when there's nothing to do. Some rooms don't even have orders yet they are popping up here. There's nothing to do so why are they showing up?"* So a table is there from its first dish until its bill is sent; a room is there only while a dish for it has not been carried out (its food is on the room's bill already); and nothing with an empty slip is there at all.
- **Food on a room's bill** can be paid before check-out, and the money is always taken at the front desk, from the booking (Sebastian, 2026-10-04). What is still unpaid at check-out is paid with the stay.
- **The restaurant sends the bill to the front desk; it does not print one or take money.** Sebastian, 2026-10-05, of the "Guest copy" and "Receive at the front desk" buttons on the order slip: *"I don't like the idea of having guest copy and receive at front desk. send bill to front desk maybe after the guests finish eating or asks for a bill."* He asked for it to follow how restaurant tills work in more developed countries. There, a bill that has been asked for is a step of its own between eating and paying, the whole house can see it, and where guests pay at a cashier the slip is closed and the table freed when the bill goes out; in a hotel a room guest's bill is closed onto their room. So "Send bill to front desk" closes the slip to more orders and hands it over, and the bill is printed at the front desk, where the printer is.
- **A walk-in diner pays at the front desk too.** Sebastian, 2026-10-04: *"The guests actually always pay at the front desk, not at the restaurant."* No money is taken on the Restaurant screen; the front desk lists the diners who have not paid. They pay the whole slip — nothing is typed and nothing is part-paid — and the receipt prints at once because they are standing there.
- **A room guest can only order once checked in.** People order when they are physically in the hotel, never over the phone.

## The till screen

Laid out in the order the counter works (the owner picked this from three drawings):

1. **A strip across the top** — the tables being served, each with what it has come to, and "New table". (It was first everyone who could be charged, every guest in the hotel included; see "Who can be charged".) No search box here; the search belongs to the menu.
2. **The bill on the left.**
3. **The menu on the right.**

- **It is used on a tablet most of the time** (Sebastian, 2026-10-04: *"the restaurant will mainly be used by tablet either landscape or portrait"*), and so is the kitchen's screen. A phone comes second.
- **No "Diners still to pay" on the Restaurant screen.** Sebastian, seeing it there, 2026-10-04: *"what is that? why is that there?"* It was what the counter chased when diners paid in the restaurant; nobody pays there now, and the front desk lists them.
- **Held upright or sideways** — *"allow portrait and landscape style for tablets."* Held sideways (or on a PC) the order slip sits beside the menu. Held upright, or on a phone, the menu takes the screen and the order is a bar docked under it that opens into the whole slip. The slip used to sit above the menu there: on a phone the first dish was a whole screen down.
- **Orders are taken on the Restaurant screen, for both kinds of guest.** A menu squeezed into the booking panel was the wrong place; the booking's panel only lists the order slips and hands staff over.
- **A room guest's balance is updated the moment food is ordered** (since 2026-10-03), so "Who owes" is right without opening the booking.

**The redesign of 2026-10-08.** Sebastian asked for the Orders screen to be *"simpler … a more visual design instead of a text heavy design and … more of a satisfying experience to use"*, and worked through it on drawings. What he ruled, in his words:

- **The tables are put away, not the menu or the slip.** He looked at the screen with the menu and the slip hidden until a table was picked: *"the menu and list shouldn't be the ones hidden. the tables should be hidden."* The line of tables across the top (1, above) is gone; the slip's title opens them, *"just below the table button"*, as one square per table.
- **The table button says when food is ready:** *"the table button should have a notification when a dish is ready to serve."*
- **No bill before the food:** *"the send bill shouldn't even be clickable if nothing has been served yet."* One served dish is enough; he was offered "every dish served" and did not ask for it.
- **"Send bill" asks where:** *"make the send bill give options like room? or front desk?"*
- **The number on a slip line takes one off**, as in the breakfast window, and the count is no longer shown on the menu: *"can we remove the number on the menu when added to the list?"*
- **The dot flies to the dish's own line** — *"make it move towards the position of the item selected."*
- **A best seller.** *"Keep track of how many times each item has been ordered and add an indicator of which item is the most popular."* He chose fine double gold rules with a diamond over medals, stars, bars and a crown (*"it's making it look less like a food item"*), wanted it *"less loud"* but *"a bit more premium"*, called it **"Best seller"** (not "House favourite"), and dropped second and third place. One dish on the whole menu — it was first one per section: *"why are there 3?"*
- **It must look like the Front desk.** The first drawings (round table tokens, big dish tiles) *"feel too simple … and it doesn't feel consistent with the front desk's design"*, so it is built from the calendar's own parts: its pills' colours and edges, its cards, its buttons.
- **No button waits for the server.** Testing it live the same day: *"the serve and send bill flashes when I click serve. can you make sure every single button uses the same method of hiding the backend interaction to make it feel more fluid?"* Serve, Send to kitchen, Send bill and starting a table now show at once and save behind, as a menu tap always did; a save that fails is taken back off the screen and said.
- **"Diners to pay" on the calendar is only for bills the restaurant has sent.** Sebastian, 2026-10-09, seeing it count a table still eating: *"this should only pop up if the restaurant actually sends it to front desk."*
- **No guide here:** *"no need for the guide in the restaurant."*
- Twelve numbered tables is a guess; he was asked how many the restaurant has and had not said.

## The menu

- **Orders are tapped, never typed.** A hand-written line hides behind "+ Something not on the menu" for the rare dish the menu lacks.
- **A charge that is not food goes on the slip too** (Sebastian, 2026-11-03): corkage, a take-out box, a broken glass. He wanted just a label and a price, with no ready-made list, and the two boxes open on the slip's own "+ Charge" line rather than in a box over it ("the design size is too big than the content it contains. keep it clean and space efficient"). A charge never goes to the kitchen.
- **It looks like the hotel's printed menu card:** *"Make it look more like a normal physical menu."* One scroll, no category buttons, name · dotted line · price, two columns.
- **Staff hold the tablet in front of the guest**, so every dish is a tall row and the whole row is tappable. A dish already on the order slip shows its count.
- **A "Find a dish" box sits above the card** because hunting one of 61 dishes is slow with a guest waiting.
- **The menu never makes the desk wait between dishes.** Sebastian, testing the till, 2026-10-04: *"every time I add an order it has to load and wait for the server to update before I can add another."* A dish is on the order slip the moment it is tapped.
- The menu lives in the database (61 items, 15 groups, typed from the laminated card) so every tablet shows the same prices. A dish is retired, never deleted, so old orders still read correctly.
- It is a staff tool. Nobody hands the tablet to a diner.

## Mistakes

- **A wrong line is simply removed**, the same way a wrong payment is. The first plan was a "correction" line; the owner changed it so there is one money rule, not two. The only thing changed on a line is its count.

## Paper

- **The kitchen gets no paper.** Its screen carries what the printed copy did: the slip's number, where the food goes, and each dish with its count. No prices.
- **A guest can ask for their order slip mid-stay.** It prints on the 58 mm roll with the prices, stamped **NOT A RECEIPT**, because no money has been received.
- A walk-in's paid order slip prints a numbered receipt.

## In the reports

- Food and bar is its own line in the Earnings Report, counted on the day each order was placed, so a lunch never reads as a room earning more.

## Why food is kept apart from the booking record

Food lines live in their own tables, never on the booking. Saving a small food line through the booking would blank other booking fields.

## The kitchen's screen

- **Each order is a checklist.** Sebastian, 2026-10-05, of the first kitchen screen: *"the kitchen feels a bit confusing, the user experience is very lacking and the UI is a bit confusing."* A tick was drawn beside every dish before anything was cooked, a tapped dish vanished, and the big button carried the same tick. Now an empty circle is a dish to cook, a tap ticks and strikes it through and it stays on the card, a second tap takes it back, and "Order ready" clears the card.
- **It follows how kitchen screens work in more developed countries**, which he asked me to look at first: a ticket is coloured by how long it has waited, one dish or the whole ticket can be marked, a wrong tap can be recalled, a sound says a new one has arrived, and everything still to cook is added up across the tickets.
- **A card leads with the table**, because that is where the food is carried.
- **The restaurant changes its own menu** (the owner, 2026-10-09: "allow the restaurant to add new items on the menu and edit the prices"). Restaurant → Menu. A dish taken off is hidden, not deleted, so old order slips keep it.

## History

- **Restaurant → History tracks what happened to each order slip** (Sebastian, 2026-11-05): *"track orders, and serves, the sent to front desk, and sent to room."* The database writes each step as it happens, so nobody records anything extra.
- **The table and the actions must not look alike**: *"make the table and actions more distinguishable."* The table is a numbered square; each step is an icon with words.
- **The latest step is at the top**: *"latest should be at the top, not bottom."*
- **No new colour meanings.** *"I want to keep a consistent color signal meaning. so I don't want the staff to be confused of what a color means because we're using it so much on other things."* Gold already means "in the kitchen" and green means "ready" or "paid", so every step in History is plain ink with its own icon.
- **Icons strong enough to read at a glance, still without colour.** After it went live he asked to *"make the icons more visible"*. Of the mock's options he took the one that is only darker, bigger and thicker, not the violet ones.
- **A row shows its three latest steps**: *"the icons should only show the 3 most recent and add a ... or something to show that there a more things that happened previously"*. The earlier ones are a count ("+3") rather than "…", because a count says how many.
- **Everything lines up**: *"make sure the text, icons, and everything is aligned or centered to make sure that it looks solid and structured."* Rows and their steps share one set of columns, with the step's icon centred under the table.

## How the restaurant really works (2026-11-09)

- **The staff take orders on paper, not on the Restaurant screen.** Sebastian, 2026-11-09: *"the flow they usually have is just take orders, write them by hand and give it to the chef, that's it."* They type the slip into the system afterwards, when they have time. On live, most slips went from opened to paid in about 30 seconds, and 5 sat served but never billed.
- **The screen added steps the paper never had**: *"I think the restaurant system is adding too many unnecessary steps and adding more bottleneck to their workflow."* Writing by hand is faster, so about a quarter of restaurant payments (29 of 110 in 30 days) were typed as an amount only in Cash Flow, with no dishes.
- **The menu is for customers, not staff**: *"the menu is worthless for the staff since menu's are supposed to be for the customers."*

## Removing a table's orders (2026-11-12)

- Sebastian, 2026-11-12: *"add a feature that allow removing orders in the tables that are in the restaurant."* One dish could already be taken off; a whole table could not (notes #21).
- Sebastian, 2026-11-13, on the front desk's order slip window: *"allow the front desk to also delete orders."* A diner's bill sent to the desk can lose a dish or all of them there, without sending it back to the restaurant.
