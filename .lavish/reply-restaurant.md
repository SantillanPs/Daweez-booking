Nothing built — three shapes for the same screen, drawn as wireframes so the layout is what you read.

**What makes it feel lazy, as I see it:** the left rail is *three cards* (charge a room / open a diner tab / open now) all doing one job — choosing who you are serving; the money is the quietest thing on screen (₱600 is smaller than the dish names); and below the bill everything is one stack — lines, total, menu, note.

- **P1 (recommended) · One job at a time: who → till.** A two-way switch, `On the room` / `Walk-in diner`, so only that job’s form is on screen. The bill becomes a header strip — **who + where + the total as the loudest figure** — with the lines under it, and the till fills everything below. The day’s takings sit top-right as a number, not a sentence.
- **P2 · Everyone open, always visible.** No cards, no switch: one strip listing every tab you are serving (rooms and diners together) each with its own total, the selected bill as a running receipt on the left, the till on the right. Fewest clicks at a busy counter — but the strip grows with the room.
- **P3 · A board of open tabs.** The screen opens as tiles, one per tab, each with its total; tap one and its bill + till slide in. Best when several tabs are open — costs one extra tap before you can take an order.

Same in all three: one tap = one line, the count badge, ✕ removes a line, food joins the room bill at check-out, a roomless diner settles at the counter, and “Print the tab” stays on the bill. No new features.
