Second: the promo price, gone permanently

Your instruction names the deeper fault. The app carries two price columns and treats the promo one as the real price — but the data has drifted, so the two disagree almost everywhere:

9 of 10 rooms disagree. Two columns for one price is two chances to quote the wrong figure — and every screen that reads one instead of the other is a place the guest is told a different number.

What “gone permanently” changes

  • Data, once: the promo figure becomes the real price — base_price = promo_price for every room and venue that has one. The two columns agree forever after.
  • The engine: calculatePricing loses its usePromo branch and reads one price. No more “charge the regular figure” path to get wrong.
  • Bookings: promo_applied stops being written and read. The column stays as history, ignored.
  • Every caller that today passes usePromo: booking.promo_applied === true — the quick view, the statement, the balance, analytics, the extend-stay panel — stops passing it.
  • Settings writes one figure, so a new price can never leave a stale second one behind.

One consequence you have to decide

Log old booking has a tick-box, “Use the price on the board today”. Unticking it prices a past paper booking at the old regular figure — the base_price — so an old log is recorded at the price the guest actually paid back then. If base_price becomes equal to the promo price, that tick-box has nothing left to switch to.

ACollapse both — one price, everywhere

base_price is set equal to the promo price. The old-log tick-box is removed: every backfilled booking is priced at today's price.

  • Simplest, and it is literally what you said.
  • You lose the ability to log a past stay at the old figure.

BOne price the app charges — keep base as the old figure

Everything the app charges uses one price. base_price survives as a dormant “old board price” that only the old-log tick-box reads.

  • You keep backfilling old stays at the old figure.
  • One column still exists that is not the price — the thing that caused this drift.
