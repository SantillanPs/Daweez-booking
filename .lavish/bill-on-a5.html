The Pension Policies is the single biggest block on the page — and you ruled it must print word for word, on every bill

It is ~1,400 characters — about 14 lines at the size it prints now. It alone is a quarter of the page. Shrinking the type is therefore not a small tweak: it is the only thing that can buy that much space, and it is exactly the text the guest is meant to be able to read before signing.

02

Three honest ways out

All three stop the cut-off. They differ in what the guest has to read and what the desk has to feed the printer.

A · my pick

One A5 sheet, printed on both sides

Nothing shrinks and nothing is cut. Front: letterhead through the totals. Back: Prepared by, Guest Signature and the policies — at the size they print today.

Cost: the printer must print both sides. A one-sided printer hands the guest two half-sheets instead of one.

B

Everything on one A5 side, shrunk

Everything drops about 30% — the 9.5px policies land near 7px, and the Amount Due figure from 22px to ~15px.

Cost: the guest signs a policy they can barely read, and every line of the bill gets tighter. It fits — that is all it does.

C

Two A5 sheets, one side each

Same split as A, but arranged for a printer that cannot print both sides: sheet 1 is the bill, sheet 2 is the policies and the signature. Nothing shrinks either — it just uses two pieces of paper.

03

One thing that is not in question

Whichever you pick, the split will be put in deliberately rather than wherever the browser happens to run out of room — which is what you are seeing now, a block sliced in half.

The agency bill is not affected. It prints no Pension Policies and no signature, so it already fits one A5 side on its own.
