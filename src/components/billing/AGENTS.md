## Work Guidance

- **A5 is the paper, and 559px is the trap (card k144, the owner's ruling, 2026-09-29)**: `PrintInvoiceModal` sets `@page { size: A5 }` — **A5 is 148mm = 559px wide, and Tailwind's `sm:` breakpoint starts at 640px.** So **any block written `grid-cols-1 sm:grid-cols-2` (or `flex-col sm:flex-row`) silently collapses to ONE COLUMN on paper**, and the whole statement had been printing as a single column because of it: the letterhead stacked, the guest fields stacked, the stay fields stacked, payment stacked over totals. That made the page **~1,050px against ~745px of usable A5** — 1.4 pages — so the bottom was cut mid-block. **Never put a viewport breakpoint on a layout that has to hold on paper.** The statement is always rendered at 576px or less (`max-w-xl`), so its two-column blocks are simply **unconditional** (`grid-cols-2`), which also makes the on-screen preview match the paper exactly. The measured fixes, all with no type shrunk: the **letterhead** is always hotel-left / bill-number-right; **Guest and Stay are TWO STACKED GROUPS — `GUEST` then `STAY` — separated by SPACE ALONE** (the owner's rulings, 2026-09-29: first *"let's establish the layout of the information … I meant like organize it"*, then *"remove the guest and stay pills, and just add a bit of space between name and room rows"*). Before this every field sat in **one flat two-column grid**, so the guest's name read beside a room number and a contact number sat under a check-out date — the reader had to work out for themselves which line was about **who** was staying and which about **the stay**. Each group is now a two-column grid of its own that closes up independently (a field the booking does not hold returns `null`), so **a field can never jump between groups**, and both columns of a group fill before the next begins — which is why grouping costs **fewer rows** than the flat grid did. **The gap between the two groups is `space-y-4` (16px): it must stay visibly wider than the 2px `gap-y-0.5` between rows inside a grid** — that gap is the whole grouping cue now that the headings are gone — **and well under a row's height**, or it reads as a hole on a sheet this dense. The two grids share one wrapper that is the single direct child of `StatementShell`, so the page's 8px rhythm still separates this block from the letterhead and the charges table. **A group with nothing in it is not rendered at all.** Everything about them lives in [StatementGuestStay](file:///c:/Users/dev4s/Documents/Programming/Daweez-booking/src/components/billing/StatementGuestStay.tsx); the **payment methods are two columns of ticks** with **Totals beside them**; and the signatures were already `grid-cols-2`. Total lands near **600px**, leaving real slack for a booking with companions, several charge rows and a long address. **The Pension Policies block is untouched** — ~1,400 characters at 9.5px, printed verbatim, and it is the single biggest block on the page, so anything that grows the page must not come out of it. **The paper's labels are SHORT, and that is what makes it fit** (the same ruling, after the owner's *"still a bit inefficient"*): a long label leaves its value too little room in a half-width column, so the value drops to a second line — **eight wrapped lines were throwing away ~220px**, more than the page's whole overflow. The printed labels are therefore **`Name` · `Address` · `Email` · `Nationality` · `Birth Date` · `Sex` · `Contact` · `Plate No.`** and **`Room No.` · `Type` · `Check In` · `Check Out` · `Standard` · `Guests`** (never `Guest's Name` / `Check In Date & Time` / `Check Out Date & Time` / `Standard Check-in / Out` / `No. of Guests` / `Contact No.` / `Email Address` / `Room Type`), a **value must never wrap**, the letterhead's name is `text-[16px] whitespace-nowrap` with the right-hand block at `text-[12px]` (at `text-lg` it needed ~240px and had ~235, so it broke after “PENSION”), **Where to pay is ONE ROW PER WAY TO PAY, at the FULL SHEET WIDTH** — a bold `Cash` / `GCash` / `BPI` label in its own **56px (`w-14`)** column with its detail beside it (`at the front desk` · `Narlina D. · 0910-7934988` · `DAWEEZ PENSION HOUSE · 5636-0544-12`), never a run-on sentence. Three faults were fixed here in turn (the owner, 2026-09-29): the single line that came first strung three routes together with `·` so the guest could not tell where one ended and the next began (*"can you fix the where to pay? it looks confusing"*); the three labelled rows that replaced it then sat in the payment block's **left column (~198px)**, where the bank row broke after the first hyphen — **a bank account number that wraps is how a guest mistypes it, so both numbers carry `whitespace-nowrap`** — and its 64px label column left a band of dead space when the longest label needs ~33px (*"can we improve how it looks?"*). The block is now a **direct child of `StatementShell`, so it spans the sheet and takes part in the page's 8px `space-y-2` rhythm**, and **it carries no dashed rule above it** — one sat a few lines under another dashed rule, and the uppercase heading already separates it. The tick list takes the bank's **short** name via `shortBankName` (`BPI Transfer`) while **Account Details keeps the full name** a guest needs in order to transfer. **Zero-value rows stay hidden** (a `Downpayment ₱0` line is worthless to the guest); the agency bill carries no policies or signature and had the same `sm:` fault on its own field grid, now fixed. **And a hidden element still takes up room**: `index.css`'s print block used to step the app aside with `visibility: hidden`, which hides the pixels but **keeps the layout** — so the statement stayed in the document flow *below* the whole hidden dashboard and printed about a third of the way down an otherwise blank A5 sheet (the owner's screenshot). `.print-slip` escaped that only because it is pinned `position: fixed; top: 0`. Everything that is not the printed document is now taken out of the flow with `display: none` (`body > *:has(.print-page)`, `:has(.print-slip)`), so the statement starts at the **top of page 1** and can still **flow onto a second page** when a booking is long enough — pinning it `fixed` like the slip would clip a long bill instead, which is worse than a gap. **And the sheet has ONE spacing rhythm, owned by the container** (the same ruling, after the owner's *"check for other parts that have massive padding"*, a full audit of every padding in the printed chain — ~**116px** was going to stacked padding — and then his last word on it: *"can you just make the padding consistent?"*). **`StatementShell`'s page sets `space-y-2`**, so every direct child — the letterhead, the bold rule, the fields, the charges table, the payment block, the Pension Policies, the signatures, the signing note — is exactly **8px** below the one before it. `BrandHeader` returns a **fragment**, so its letterhead div and its rule div are direct children too and join the same rhythm; that is what finally removed the **46px of air** the owner circled around the rule under the letterhead (`pb-4` + `my-2` + the field block's `py-3`). **The blocks therefore carry no vertical padding or margin of their own** — `StatementChargesTable` in particular brings **none**, where its old `py-3` stacked with the `py-3` above and below it, three paddings at a boundary that needs 8, which is why the table read as floating. A child that draws its own rule keeps only the padding **inside** it (`pt-2`, or `pt-3` for the signature block, which needs writing room) — the 8px *above* the rule comes from the rhythm, and that is also how the bold line above the payment method got the little bit of space the owner asked for. `Line` stays `pb-px` with `gap-y-0.5`; the table's inner `py-1` and the plan line's `mt-2` are **left alone**, because a gap under 12px inside a block is doing real work. **If a block ever grows its own vertical padding, this rhythm is gone and the drift starts again.** The **agency bill uses the identical rhythm** (it shares `StatementShell`, and its own two field columns are `space-y-1`), so the two papers are one spacing system rather than two. **The statement's own colours are forced onto the paper**: a browser throws backgrounds away when printing unless told otherwise, so the charges table's `bg-paper-100` header and its `bg-paper-200` band rows came off the printer **white** while the screen preview showed them beige (the owner's screenshot, *"the table colors aren't showing"*). `index.css`'s print block gives `.print-page, .print-page *` the `-webkit-print-color-adjust: exact` / `print-color-adjust: exact` pair. **It is deliberately NOT applied to `.print-slip`** — the 58 mm thermal roll is authored black-on-white on purpose (its printer is 1-bit, so a grey fill only dithers into fuzz) and forcing exact colours there would turn a light background into noise on the paper. If a statement still prints white, the print dialog's own **Background graphics** setting is what is suppressing it — that is a browser setting, not a stylesheet one, and a design that must never depend on it should say its table bands with **borders and weight** instead of a fill.
