## Work Guidance

- **Group Selection Mode**: When booking multiple units in Group Selection Mode, each unit has its own check-in/out date range selected sequentially on the timeline grid (2 clicks per unit: check-in → check-out). Toggling or selecting ranges runs immediate collision checks; conflicts trigger warnings but do not clear the active selection list. Picks accumulate silently. **The booking actions live in the grid's own action bar, not the toolbar** (the owner's design, see the next bullet): it appears once something is picked and carries the buttons that suit the pick. Launching any booking action — **New booking**, **Corporate**, the **short-stay hours** or **Log old booking** — clears the picked selections off the calendar so they don't stay highlighted after.

- **The action bar on the picked cell (the owner's design)**: there are **no booking buttons in the toolbar any more** — `CalendarToolbar` keeps only the window title, ‹ ›, Today, the date box and the **Log old booking** icon. Instead `TimelineGrid` draws a bar **pinned to the cell the desk picked**: it finds `[data-unit][data-day]` — **every clickable cell carries those two markers, including the gold “In” cell a single picked day renders as** (that one was missing them, which is why the Short stay button never appeared for a single day) — and sits **just above that row** (one bar height plus a small gap), inside the scroller so it travels with the grid on either scroll; for the top rows, where the sticky day header is, it drops **below** the row instead. Its left edge starts **two day columns to the left of the picked day** (the owner's ask) — the day width is measured from the `th[data-day]` headers rather than hard-coded, and the shift is clamped at the **first** day column so a pick near the left edge, which is common because the window opens on today, can never slide it over the sticky room-name column. Sitting it *on* the row was the owner's next catch — *"it's covering the dates of the room I chose"* — so the room's own dates must stay visible. The first version lined up with the *date* but sat at the top of the grid, and the owner's verdict was *"I chose room 6 and the buttons are far from the date and room selected"* — the bar must be beside the **room as well as the date**. **What it offers depends on the pick**: **one day** → the three short-stay hours `3h · 6h · 12h` at **the picked room's own board prices** (the owner's ask: the old single `Short stay` button only opened the form, which then asked the same question again — an hour with no price on the room is not tappable and says where to set it in its tooltip, and a **venue** never shows hour buttons, only *Tap the check-out day for a normal stay*), **22 hours is deliberately not there** because it IS the room's normal price, i.e. an ordinary overnight stay booked from a range; **a range** → `New booking` + `Corporate`; and Cancel on both. **Cancel goes through `onClearSelection`**, which clears the single day *and* the finished range — the first version only cleared the single day, so Cancel did nothing after a range was picked. The point of all of it is that nothing is greyed out and the rule teaches itself — *pick the dates first*. `Log old booking` stays in the toolbar because it is a back-office job, not a stay. The bar is positioned with CSS custom properties (`--bar-left`, `--bar-top`) rather than raw inline styles, which `shadcn/no-inline-styles` rejects.

- **Short stays (the printed rate board)**: from the action bar, **tapping `3h`, `6h` or `12h`** on a single picked day IS the whole booking — pressing it opens the form with the room, the date and those hours already in it, and `check_out` filled in as the next day for the desk, because a short stay is one day (the owner's complaint was having to pick two dates: *"I need to select 2 dates. I can't only select 1"*, and his follow-up was that the single `Short stay` button made the desk answer the same question twice). A booking carrying `stay_hours` (3/6/12/22) was sold for hours instead of a night, but it **blocks the whole day** — housekeeping cleans the room afterwards, so it is never resold the same day — which is why availability, the grid and the bill need no other special case. `TimelineCell` carries the short stay as its own tight two-line block — the guest's name over `out 1:12 PM`, or over `3-hour stay` before check-in (see the clock bullet) — its **memo comparator carries `stay_hours`** and `isShortStayDue`, and the tooltip reads `Short stay · 3 hours · out by 1:12 PM`. `ExtendStayModal` records **no early or late hours** for such a booking (a 10am arrival for a three-hour stay is not “four hours early”), with `chargeableEarlyHours` returning 0 for it.

- **The short-stay clock (the owner's ruling, and the shape he asked for)**: **no countdown is ever shown** — the app states the TIME the room is free. All of it is worked out in `utils/shortStay.ts` (`stayHoursOf`, `shortStayEnd`, `clockLabel`, `shortStayLine`), which reads the booking's `stay_hours` and the recorded arrival; the clock **starts at the Check in press**, so nothing exists to show before it. Three places read it:
  - **The calendar block** (`TimelineCell`) is a tight two-line pill: the guest's name with the payment dot on top, then the fact the desk acts on — `3-hour stay` before the desk has checked the guest in, `out 1:12 PM` after. The day columns are **84 px** so a real name fits above the full time (the owner set the width himself: wider for the phrase, then a little narrower because the columns read too wide) — never shorter than this, or `SEBASTIAN` and `out 8:02 AM` start to truncate. Line 2 never repeats "short stay" (the gold block and the tooltip already say it) and never counts down. At 36 px (`h-9`) the block is barely taller than an ordinary one. A block whose hours have run out turns **red** (rose fill, `isShortStayDue`) — the cell comparator's newest field.
  - **`ShortStayClock`** draws the panel's own line — gold `10:12 AM → 1:12 PM · 3-hour stay`, red `Time is up at 1:12 PM — the room is due for cleaning` — and renders nothing for an ordinary stay or before check-in. `due` is handed in from `CalendarTab` (the page already ticks the clock — that tick is what pops the panel open), so the panel runs no clock of its own: reading `Date.now()` while rendering is forbidden.
  - **`CalendarTab`** owns the **pop-out**: every 15 s it collects the short stays past their hours and, when the desk is free (no booking form, corporate form, old-booking modal or other quick view open), opens the quick view on the first one nobody has dealt with. Due rooms are opened **one after another**, never stacked — closing the panel lets the next one open — and a panel the desk has closed is **never reopened** (the block simply stays red until they tap it). Past the hours the app **only tells the desk**: it adds no money on its own.
- **Old Booking Backfill (paper logs)**: Use the calendar toolbar's **Log old booking** to import a historical paper booking. The system still assigns its own sequential `invoice_number` (GRF-YYYYMM-NNNN), and the original paper log number + date are stored in `reference_number`/`registered_on` and included in the bookings-list search (so staff find a booking by guest name, the system number, or the old paper number). What was paid is recorded via `downpayment_paid` / `balance_due` / `payment_status` and a `payment_records` entry. The form accepts **multiple rooms/venues** at once (pre-filled from the calendar selection). The **Total is auto-computed** across all selected units (each unit's nightly rate × its nights), priced at **the price on the board today** — one small checkbox, ticked by default, switches it to the **older regular figure** for a log written before the price changed; that choice is passed through and stored as `promo_applied`, so a later read never re-prices the log. Staff only type the amount actually paid, which is split proportionally across the bookings; contact number is optional (a paper log may have only a guest name).
